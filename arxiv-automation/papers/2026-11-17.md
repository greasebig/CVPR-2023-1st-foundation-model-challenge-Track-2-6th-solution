## 1. Reference Forcing: Long Video Generation with Region-Guided Drift Correction

**中文标题**: Reference Forcing: Long Video Generation with Region-Guided Drift Correction

**作者**: Sheng Zhao, Haitian Zheng, Weikai Lin, Junjie Zhao, Yuhao Zhu

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21559v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21559v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21559v1)

**项目主页**: https://zhaosheng-thu.github.io/reference-force/

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Long video generation should keep the subject and background consistent while supporting rich, natural motion. However, autoregressive models are often distilled with distribution matching distillation (DMD) over short windows, which does not train them to keep this consistency over long ranges. As a result, the subject and background can drift over time. Methods that manage the key-value (KV) cache retain or retrieve history to reduce drift. Yet this history may itself have drifted. We propose Reference Forcing, which uses the initial frames as an appearance reference. We first build a teacher that uses attention masks to force each region of a new frame to attend to the same region in the initial frames. The teacher finds these regions with off-the-shelf segmentation models. We then distill this behavior into a separate attention path of the student, which needs no external models at inference. We further introduce value correction, which corrects drifted values in the KV cache toward the matching values of drift-free initial frames. Experiments show that Reference Forcing achieves state-of-the-art subject and background consistency among long-video methods on different base models, and its advantage grows with video length. Ablations show both components are needed to gain consistency without losing motion. More visualizations and code are available at https://zhaosheng-thu.github.io/reference-force/

</details>

## 2. BLIP-Live: Balancing Timeliness & Firing Precision in Proactive Streaming Video Agents

**中文标题**: BLIP-Live: Balancing Timeliness & Firing Precision in Proactive Streaming Video Agents

**作者**: Kanchana Ranasinghe, Honglu Zhou, Dominick Reilly, Le Xue, Shusheng Yang et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21553v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21553v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21553v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Streaming video understanding is becoming increasingly important for real-world interactive systems. Such systems must be proactive for natural and useful human interaction: they should respond as soon as sufficient evidence becomes available while avoiding premature, redundant, or incorrect responses. We present BLIP-Live, a framework unifying data, metrics, harness, and training around a shared formulation of proactivity as effective causal response decisions balancing the two competing objectives of responding vs silence. First, we construct BLIP-Live-780K dataset containing human annotations of proactive response content and timing, which defines this desired behavior. We then propose PAUC-F, a metric to effectively measure our desired proactive behavior. PAUC-F combines existing response timeliness metrics with firing precision to reward early useful responses without rewarding excessive firing. With our desired behavior defined and measurable, we finally investigate model-agnostic inference harnesses that draw out proactive streaming capabilities of offline vision language models (VLMs), together with training pipelines that learn to further improve their proactive responsiveness. Across both settings, we find that combinations of sliding visual windows, online interaction memory, and structured response outputs consistently yield strongest proactive performance. Our experiments result in two models, with our BLIP-Live-Qwen model achieving state-of-the-art performance across four proactive streaming benchmarks, with gains up to 44% over baselines and outperforming multiple closed API models. Our code and data will be released publicly.

</details>

## 3. DuoFlow: Towards Synchronized Speech and Motion Generation with Fine-Grained Control

**中文标题**: DuoFlow: Towards Synchronized Speech and Motion Generation with Fine-Grained Control

**作者**: Jun Dong, Ronghui Li, Zhongyuan Hu, Yunqi Zhao, Lijie Guo et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21530v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21530v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21530v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

An embodied speaker's expressiveness depends not only on what is said, but also on how the voice sounds and how the body moves. However, existing joint speech-motion generation models are largely driven implicitly by transcript semantics or intermediate speech representations, offering little explicit control over motion hierarchy, body parts, or vocal style. To bridge this gap, we construct MotionSpeech, a structured benchmark for controllable joint speech-motion generation that includes transcripts, voice-style descriptions, and motion conditions at two hierarchical levels. Building on this benchmark, we propose DuoFlow, a dual-stream latent flow transformer that jointly denoises speech and motion under a shared noise level and a unified second-based temporal coordinate. Modality-specific residual connectors inject structured conditions into the corresponding streams, layer-wise bidirectional cross-modal attention coordinates their generation, and a monotonic phoneme pathway combines normalized progress anchors with CTC supervision to strengthen speech temporal modeling. DuoFlow provides a unified interface for fine-grained motion and vocal-style control and demonstrates the ability to synthesize high-quality, synchronized, and instruction-consistent speech-motion sequences.

</details>

## 4. VideoNexus: Video Perception Beyond the Reasoning Context

**中文标题**: VideoNexus: Video Perception Beyond the Reasoning Context

**作者**: Yancheng Long, Xuanyu Zheng, Wei Chen, Luoyuan Zhang, Haoyang Xu et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21392v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21392v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21392v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Understanding long videos requires locating evidence, resolving visual details, and integrating observations into an answer. Even when relevant evidence is present, performing these operations within long, dense visual sequences burdens both perception and reasoning. Video agents enable selective inspection, but returning frames to the planner still accumulates visual information in its reasoning context. Delegating perception reduces this burden, yet sparse sampling can miss decisive details, and per-frame descriptions can preserve context growth. We introduce VideoNexus, a training-free framework that separates detailed video perception from evidence-driven planning and reasoning. The planner controls where to inspect, what to resolve, and how densely to sample. Perception sub-agents process native audio-video in independent contexts and return compact, temporally grounded evidence for iterative search and verification. Using Gemini 3.8 Flash for both planning and perception, VideoNexus achieves 88.6% accuracy on full LVBench, exceeding same-backbone static ingestion and built-in agentic processing by 3.6 and 1.1 points, respectively, at 44% of the latter's measured API cost. A paired LVBench subset ablation shows lower planner input and higher accuracy. With Kimi K3 as the shared backbone, VideoNexus improves over the official Video-GAIA ReAct harness by 8.5 points in our evaluation. These results support compact evidence exchange for combining detailed observation with efficient planning and reasoning.

</details>

## 5. WeVisForensics: Self-Evolving Agent for Omni AI-Generated Video Detection

**中文标题**: WeVisForensics: Self-Evolving Agent for Omni AI-Generated Video Detection

**作者**: Yipeng Du, Yuxi Li, Ying Zhang, Chong Sun, Chen Li et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21373v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21373v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21373v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.AI

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Recent advances in video generation call for reliable detection grounded in explicit forensic evidence. However, current methods primarily depend on single-pass chain-of-thought reasoning that overlooks brief or localized artifacts, or on predefined multi-agent workflows that offer limited flexibility in selecting relevant inspections. Even with adaptive investigation, optimizing the investigation policy alone does not directly improve the forensic capabilities of fixed experts. We introduce WeVisForensics, an agentic framework in which a main investigator agent coordinates a self-evolving library of forensic inspector sub-agents spanning visual and audio cues. The investigator learns which artifact type to examine, over which window, and when to conclude, and these interactions provide shared feedback for both levels of agents. Our dual-timescale policy optimization strategy, DTPO, closes this loop through fast reinforcement learning of the investigator and slower refinement of the inspectors, guided by annotated artifact intervals, to report subtle artifacts they once dismissed. Furthermore, we curate ForenCue-30K, combining role-specific forensic annotations and interaction trajectories to supervise individual inspections and their coordination, with an accompanying reinforcement-learning pool. Experiments show that WeVisForensics averages 95.2% detection accuracy across in-domain, out-of-domain and agentic benchmarks, 9.0% above the strongest prior detector, while grounding its verdicts in localized evidence.

</details>

## 7. MovieRoll: A Systematic Framework for Rolling Out Long-Form Videos

**中文标题**: MovieRoll: A Systematic Framework for Rolling Out Long-Form Videos

**作者**: Yunyang Ge, Weijie Kong, Ryan Xu, Yue Wu, Qi Tian et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21339v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21339v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21339v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Streaming video generation must sustain quality beyond the training horizon while handling self-generated history and growing cache costs. To address these challenges, we present MovieRoll, a base-to-distilled framework that connects streaming fine-tuning, long-rollout distillation, and long-horizon inference. We first establish a robust multi-step base through Window-wise Teacher Forcing (WinTF), which jointly denoises chunks at progressive noise levels with bidirectional attention within each window and causal access to preceding history. To retain historical context efficiently, hierarchical KV-cache compression (HieraKV) exploits layer-wise attention patterns, retaining full history in shallow layers and sink--recent history in deeper layers. This reduces cache memory by up to 78% while improving generation quality. Building on this base, cumulative long tuning with distribution matching distillation (CuLT-DMD) holds parameters fixed throughout each long self-rollout and averages local gradients before updating. Each update therefore combines supervision across temporal positions while retaining only one local computation graph, enabling efficient long-rollout training of a few-step generator. To extend generation beyond the training horizon, KV recomputation bounds context for both models, while guidance annealing (AnnealCFG) reduces guidance toward the clean endpoint to stabilize MovieRoll-Base. Complementing these modeling advances, FBCA attention and PS-VAE decoding support efficient training and streaming output. On HeliosBench, MovieRoll-Base remains competitive at 30s after training on videos of up to 15s, and MovieRoll-Distilled achieves the highest total scores among the compared methods across single- and multi-event evaluations up to 60s, combining sustained naturalness with active motion.

</details>

## 8. Scribble Once, Segment the Sequence: Bidirectional Temporal Consistency for Weakly Supervised Segmentation in Echocardiography Sequences

**中文标题**: Scribble Once, Segment the Sequence: Bidirectional Temporal Consistency for Weakly Supervised Segmentation in Echocardiography Sequences

**作者**: Vincent Li Weixin, Chen Jingkun, Vicente Grau

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21137v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21137v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21137v1)

**类别**: eess.IV, cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Temporal medical image segmentation relies on pixel-wise annotations, which are prohibitively expensive for long image sequences. Existing weakly-supervised methods reduce the annotation burden per frame but still require repeated annotations across the sequence. Furthermore, sparse supervision can result in temporally unstable representations. Addressing both challenges requires a model that can leverage strong spatial priors from large-scale pretraining while minimizing reliance on dense annotations. Frozen 2D vision foundation models provide a promising basis for this by offering transferable spatial representations with low trainable parameters. However, their lack of explicit temporal modeling limits their ability to propagate reliable information across anatomical sequences. To address this, we present a weakly-supervised framework for temporal segmentation from a single scribble-annotated frame, with all remaining frames left unlabeled. The framework combines parameter-efficient adaptation of DINOv3 encoders present in the mean-teacher architecture. Furthermore, a set of complementary losses, including a temporal loss helps generate reliable segmentation priors for the unlabeled frames. This formulation decouples annotation costs from sequence length while retaining the transferable spatial knowledge of the pretrained encoder. Extensive experiments on the publicly available CAMUS and CardiacNet datasets demonstrate that our approach outperforms other scribble-supervised methods. These results show that accurate and temporally consistent segmentation can be achieved from extremely sparse supervision, substantially reducing annotation requirements for temporal echocardiographic segmentation and providing a practical framework for scalable clinical deployment.

</details>

## 9. Region-VAD: Video Anomaly Localization and Detection via Region Selection

**中文标题**: Region-VAD: Video Anomaly Localization and Detection via Region Selection

**作者**: Giacomo D'Amicantonio

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21132v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21132v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21132v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Anomalies are local events, yet weakly supervised video anomaly detection scores them globally. Most methods pool an entire frame into a single vector, rank frames by that vector, and derive a location afterwards as a saliency map of the score. Full-frame pooling mixes the anomaly with its background, so a detector can reach competitive benchmark scores from scene context without being able to show where in the scene the anomaly occurs. We introduce Region-VAD, which first selects a region and then detects the anomaly from that region alone. Region-VAD selects an explicit rectangle over the tokens of a frozen foundation model and scores anomalousness only from the tokens inside the box, through attention pooling followed by a standard temporal head. The rectangle is not learned, it is the box in which the frozen tokens change most between consecutive segments, in all four feature projections of the encoder, and it is found by exhaustive search over the rectangles of the token grid. Only the pooling and the temporal head are trained, from video-level labels without boxes, masks or frame-level annotations, and no trained component reads a token outside the selected region. Experiments on five datasets, with one localizer configuration shared by all datasets and backbones, show that Region-VAD achieves state-of-the-art anomaly localization on UCF-Crime and MSAD at competitive detection performance.

</details>

## 10. VAE Rhythm: Temporal Decoding Traces for AI-Generated Video Detection

**中文标题**: VAE Rhythm: Temporal Decoding Traces for AI-Generated Video Detection

**作者**: Ke Sun, Shuwei Li, Haiyang Mei, Mike Zheng Shou

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.21114v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21114v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21114v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Visible artifacts are an unreliable cue for detecting generated videos, because newer generators learn to remove them. We instead detect a trace left by the generation pipeline itself. Most video generators decode latents with a 3D causal VAE that groups consecutive frames in time, so the fidelity of generated frames rises and falls periodically with their position in a group. We call this periodic trace VAE Rhythm. Real videos are captured frame by frame and do not have it. To reveal it without training, we propose a phase scan. The scan reconstructs a clip with an off-the-shelf VAE from successive start offsets and measures the periodicity of the reconstruction error across these offsets. We find VAE Rhythm in most of the 39 variants of 31 generators we test, including the closed-source Sora, Kling 3.0 and Seedance 2.0. We use the rhythm as a zero-shot score, as a plug-in for existing detectors and as a data engine that pairs real videos with their VAE reconstructions to train our detector CaDet. On DF26, the training-free rhythm score outperforms all 15 retrained baselines, and as a plug-in it improves all 19 tested detectors by 6.7% to 23.7% AUROC. CaDet achieves the best AUROC on AIGVDBench, DF26 and three 2026 closed-source models, and its variant trained without any generated video still exceeds 90% on AIGVDBench.

</details>

## 11. MetricHand: Geometry-Aware Flow Matching for 4D Hand Reconstruction in Metric World Space

**作者**: Guoqiang Liang, Yiqi Lin, Shangchen Zhou, Wenzheng Zeng, Yanzhe Chen et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.20741v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20741v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20741v1)

**类别**: cs.RO, cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Robotic reward models (RRMs) use foundation models to replace per-task reward engineering with reusable, task-conditioned evaluation. Yet under one name, their outputs answer different questions, from whether a task succeeded to which execution is better. This survey organizes 114 methods into five semantic families by the question their native outputs answer: Outcome, Absolute Progress, Relative, Temporal Value, and Hybrid. Within this taxonomy, we review how model design determines what an evaluator can judge, how raw experience becomes supervision, and how existing evaluation practice tests the result. The review exposes a gap: agreement with demonstration order and downstream policy gains do not show whether a reward tracks task fulfillment. We therefore introduce RRM-Bench-1K, 1,117 real-robot videos of closed-loop executions with task-state and camera-evidence labels, and four contrast suites that separate the credited condition from its usual cues. A unified study of 27 evaluators shows that the strongest final scores rank whole executions well enough for selection and filtering. Dense credit, however, follows the progression of an execution more closely than its task state, and no evaluator signals when the decisive view is missing. How much fidelity a reward model needs therefore depends on the decision it drives. We outline directions from real-failure supervision to metrics that separate task state from chronology, and open-source RRM-Hub, a toolbox in which 14 existing RRMs can be readily applied and new evaluators tested on the same capabilities.

**作者**: Hengrui Liu, Wenbo Xu, Ziqi Sheng, Wei lu

**发布日期**: 2026-11-16

**arXiv ID**: [2611.20704v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20704v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20704v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have achieved strong performance in generation based video editing, but suffer from high inference latency due to the long token sequences formed by conditioning inputs and generated videos. Although training-free sparse attention reduces this cost, existing methods typically apply a fixed retention ratio or a single threshold shared across all tokens. In this work, we view video editing as conditional generation and identify a key insight: attention demand varies significantly both across different key sources (e.g., source videos, reference images, audio, text, and generated videos) and across queries within each source. Motivated by this, we propose QASK-Edit (Query-Adaptive Sparsity over Key Sources for Video Editing), a training-free sparse attention framework that allocates computation independently for each query and key source. Specifically, for each query, QASK-Edit considers both the attention mass and approximation risk of each block. It then evaluates candidate routing thresholds against each key source's risk budget, choosing exact computation or approximations to dynamically adjust sparsity for each query. Extensive experiments on three video editing models demonstrate that QASK-Edit achieves up to $1.46\times$ end-to-end speedup over dense attention pipeline while preserving both editing quality and unedited region fidelity. Furthermore, QASK-Edit generalizes directly to Reference-to-Video generation, delivering up to $1.36\times$ speedup while maintaining generation quality.

## 62. The Flip Test: Video World Models Fail to Learn the World State

**中文标题**: The Flip Test: Video World Models Fail to Learn the World State

**作者**: Mi Luo, Lijun Yu, Adams Wei Yu, Ang Cao, Baochen Sun et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-15

**arXiv ID**: [2611.19822v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19822v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.AI, cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Video world models promise to predict the future of a scene from its present. Do current models keep this promise? We probe this question counterfactually. When a small change to the present flips the future, such as a box resting a few millimeters before or past a table edge, a model that tracks the state of the world must flip its prediction too. We build FlipTest, a three-level hierarchy of increasingly strict tests over 1,680 initial frames from 10 simulated and 15 real-world tasks. At the base, visual minimal pairs, two nearly identical frames whose true futures differ, test whether predictions change across the boundary; matched controls go further and test whether they change only where the future does; and dense sweeps, the strictest level, locate exactly where they change. All 11 video world models we evaluate fall short. The best averages 18.3% pair accuracy over real and simulated scenes, below the 25% of random guessing. Models flip as often on steps that leave the future unchanged as on steps that change it, and their response stays flat across the sweeps. On the same frames, a vision-language model is selective and traces a near S-shaped curve near the boundary, and a coding agent that measures the image comes close to the true boundary. This implies that the deciding information is in the frame, and video models just miss it. We therefore urge the community to rethink world modeling as pure video prediction. Next-frame prediction must do more than draw plausible pixels; it must also identify the causal states that control future dynamics.

To combine general-purpose manipulation capabilities with task-specific fine-grained control, while enabling effective coordination between generalist and specialized policies throughout multi-stage manipulation tasks, this paper proposes MARS (Modular Atomic-skill Routing System), a dynamic routing framework for reinforcement learning-based atomic skills. MARS integrates a frozen generalist VLA policy with multiple independently trained atomic skill policies. Using a shared RL Token state representation and the reference action of VLA, specialized control policies are optimized through reinforcement learning for different cr`itical manipulation stages. For high-level policy selection, we develop a dynamic router that integrates temporal context and skill semantics. The router employs Mamba-based recurrent encoding and a historical feature window to capture state evolution during manipulation. Skill descriptions of candidate policies are encoded into semantic queries, and attention mechanisms are used to establish correspondences between skill semantics and temporal state features. Experimental results show that MARS achieves an average Macro-F1 score of 90.47% across four LIBERO task suites under fixed-trajectory evaluation, outperforming an MLP-based router by 7.08 percentage points. In the simulated Tube Transport task, the full-task success rate increases from 20.04% to 85.67%. These results demonstrate that stage-specific reinforcement learning and temporal state-aware dynamic policy routing can improve multi-policy coordination and overall task execution performance in multi-stage robotic manipulation.
