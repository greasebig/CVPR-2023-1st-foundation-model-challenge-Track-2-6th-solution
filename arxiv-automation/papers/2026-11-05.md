## 1. Motion-guided Contrastive Regularization For Latent World Models

**中文标题**: Motion-guided Contrastive Regularization For Latent World Models

**作者**: Yiwen Chen, Qiqi Hou, Jisoo Jeong, Yinhao Zhu, Huaizu Jiang et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06901v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06901v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06901v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

JEPA-style world models learn action-conditioned latent dynamics without reconstructing observations, but distributional regularization alone does not ensure that their representations preserve information needed for control. We identify static collapse, a failure mode in which the encoder preserves episode-specific static content while suppressing manipulation-relevant state changes. Because static content is temporally predictable yet diverse across episodes, this failure can persist despite regularizers such as SIGReg. A controlled study on OGBench-Cube shows that introducing randomized backgrounds substantially degrades planning performance while leaving the underlying manipulation task unchanged. To address this problem, we propose motion-guided contrastive regularization. Our approach compares predicted latent transitions with transitions to candidate observations from the same episode, reducing the usefulness of static background identity for distinguishing action outcomes. Optical-flow similarities provide soft contrastive targets, allowing candidates with similar motion to receive similar supervision. Combined with latent prediction and SIGReg, our objective encourages representations to preserve within-episode state distinctions and similarities between related transitions. Our method improves planning success over LeWorldModel (LeWM) from 52% to 78% on OGBench-Cube with randomized backgrounds and from 14% to 54% on LIBERO-Object, while maintaining comparable performance on four other visual-control benchmarks.

</details>

## 2. PixelSpline: Continuous Space-Time Video Super-Resolution via Endpoint-Anchored Shared Pixel Trajectories

**中文标题**: PixelSpline: Continuous Space-Time Video Super-Resolution via Endpoint-Anchored Shared Pixel Trajectories

**作者**: Wanqi Yuan, Nianyi Li

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06870v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06870v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06870v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Existing continuous space-time video super-resolution methods often infer motion from only two endpoint frames and model the two motion directions independently, making them vulnerable to ambiguous correspondence and inconsistent trajectories. We introduce a continuous STVSR framework that combines long-context encoding, continuous HR feature lifting, and an endpoint-anchored PixelSpline. Long-range observations provide more reliable endpoint representations, while continuous upsampling constructs detailed appearance features directly on the requested HR grid. PixelSpline explicitly models each pixel's motion as a single continuous trajectory shared by both endpoints. Using this trajectory, our method warps endpoint colors into a coarse intermediate frame and aligns the HR features for residual reconstruction. Experiments across spatial and temporal upsampling settings demonstrate that the proposed framework offers a favorable quality--efficiency trade-off.

</details>

## 3. Visual Token Routing: Learning to keep tokens that matter

**中文标题**: Visual Token Routing: Learning to keep tokens that matter

**作者**: Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06854v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06854v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06854v1)

**项目主页**: https://pranjal2041.github.io/vtr

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.LG

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Computer-use agents (CUAs) have recently achieved superhuman performance on several long-horizon benchmarks. However, their computational cost limits their use in long-horizon tasks and real-time domains such as video games. A key bottleneck is representing each screenshot as thousands of visual tokens, which accumulate into long contexts as agents reason over extended interaction histories. We observe that most of these visual tokens are either semantically irrelevant to the agent's next action or temporally redundant across screenshots. Building on this observation, we introduce Visual Token Routing (VTR), a joint training framework for learning sparse visual representations that preserve the information needed for action prediction. In VTR, we train the vision-language model together with a lightweight spatio-temporal router that examines each image region in the current and previous screenshots and decides whether its visual token enters the language model. We optimize the router using an adaptive loss to keep the number of visual tokens as small as possible while minimizing performance degradation. With just 2,000 training steps, VTR matches the performance of the full-resolution model on OSWorld, CUA-World, and video game action prediction, while using 2.5-5.8x fewer visual tokens. These savings reduce inference cost by up to 3.3x and speed up prefill by up to 2.9x. VTR also outperforms lowering the image resolution and other baselines for dropping tokens, by up to 12.8 points on OSWorld. Overall, VTR enables computer-use agents to process only the visual tokens they need, allowing them to be used in long-horizon tasks and real-time domains at a fraction of the computational cost. Code and models are available at https://pranjal2041.github.io/vtr

</details>

## 4. VA-TDM: A Unified View for Improving Few-Step Diffusion Models by Velocity Adversarial Distillation

**中文标题**: VA-TDM: A Unified View for Improving Few-Step Diffusion Models by Velocity Adversarial Distillation

**作者**: Yihong Luo, Tianyu Pang, Tianyang Hu

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06796v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06796v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06796v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Few-step distillation offers efficient generation with diffusion and flow models, but preserving quality with only a few sampling steps remains challenging. Distribution-matching methods via score distillation provide a strong foundation, yet they rely on accurately tracking the student's changing distribution. At intermediate generation steps in few-step sampling, student-score estimation can be difficult, and commonly used estimators may describe a different distribution from the one used to update the student. Flow matching allows a more flexible approach to supervision through velocity fields constructed from the student's sampling process. Building on this perspective, we introduce Velocity Adversarial Distillation (VAD), which uses a learned critic to compare student and teacher fields and turn their disagreement into feedback for learning. VAD unifies score distillation, Fisher-based matching, and moment matching, also explaining existing heuristic MMD updates and delivering new training objectives. We further develop VA-TDM, which follows trajectory distribution matching: providing supervision for partial denoising along the student trajectory. Beyond this, VA-TDM combines transition-aware online velocity learning and dual-form VAD supervision across intermediate student transitions. Extensive results show that VA-TDM is powerful for learning few-step generators, achieving state-of-the-art four-step performance in both text-to-image and text-to-video generation across various backbones and scaling effectively to 33B MiniMax-H3. In particular, our VA-TDM-H3 notably outperforms the few-step Fast-H3 and is closer to the original many-step H3 according to human preference.

</details>

## 5. EvoWorld: Towards Out-of-Sight World Evolution in Generative Video Models

**中文标题**: EvoWorld: Towards Out-of-Sight World Evolution in Generative Video Models

**作者**: Yequan Bie, Dengyang Jiang, Zhixuan Chen, Xiaozhuan Liang, Haoxuan Che et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06748v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06748v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06748v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.AI

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Generative video models are increasingly used as visual world simulators, yet their primary strength lies in rendering plausible pixels rather than reasoning about how a world should evolve. Coherent world evolution requires more than visual fidelity: when an event occurs, a model must infer its consequences for the entities in the world and then render the resulting state. For example, after a wild beast appears, the model should not only depict the wild beast, but also infer whether a nearby person should flee, take cover, or fight back based on the realized scene. Existing diffusion-based video models lack an explicit mechanism for maintaining world state and reasoning over such event-conditioned consequences, often producing visually plausible but behaviorally static or causally inconsistent outputs. To make this reasoning gap concrete and measurable, we propose a new problem, Out-of-Sight Causal Evolution, which requires a model to render an event, reason about its consequences for the evolving world state, and reveal a causally plausible outcome as the world continues to evolve, even out of sight. As a baseline reference solution, we introduce EvoWorld, a brain-renderer collaboration framework in which a vision-language model serves as the world brain: it observes generated segments, maintains an evolving account of the world, and reasons about plausible state transitions, while a segment-autoregressive video renderer realizes them visually. We further introduce EvoWorld-Data and EvoWorld-Bench for training and evaluating event realization, causal reactions, and revisit consistency. Experiments show that EvoWorld substantially improves event-conditioned causal evolution over recent models while preserving visual quality and consistency, establishing a reference point for reasoning-guided, autonomous world evolution beyond the current field of view.

</details>

## 6. Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit

**中文标题**: Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit

**作者**: Shuai Dong, Kanghui Tian, Linghan Chen, Wenqi Shao, Hongrui Wu et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06728v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06728v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06728v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.CL, cs.LG

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Streaming video understanding requires models to repeatedly decide whether to respond or continue observing. Recently, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has been applied to this setting, with existing approaches emphasizing reward design while retaining a single trajectory-level advantage across turns. Such shared credit can reinforce redundant replies after earlier successes and discourage useful follow-ups after earlier failures. We propose TurnWise, a turn-level GRPO framework that aligns learning with individual streaming decisions. TurnWise addresses the mismatch between trajectory-level feedback and turn-level interaction, promoting informative responses and reducing redundancy. Specifically, turn-level credit assignment uses silence-completed prefix scoring to construct group-relative advantages from each decision's remaining return, separating its learning signal from earlier rewards. Turn-mean policy optimization averages token losses within each turn and then across turns, giving response and waiting decisions equal weight regardless of output length. These complementary designs improve streaming policy learning without a learned critic or additional rollouts. Extensive experiments show our method outperforms prior RL approaches for streaming video understanding.

</details>

## 7. Sekai: Incremental Event Memory for Generalized Reasoning Video Segmentation

**中文标题**: Sekai: Incremental Event Memory for Generalized Reasoning Video Segmentation

**作者**: Zhengtao Liu, Jinrong Xu, Shiu-Hong Kao, Huixin Sun, Yicong Li

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06685v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06685v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06685v1)

**代码仓库**: https://github.com/zenastangbi/Sekai.

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Online reasoning video segmentation (Online RVS) requires a model to segment query-referred objects as a video streams in, using only the frames observed so far. Existing formulations largely assume a fixed target set throughout the video, overlooking a fundamental property of streaming reasoning: as new events occur, the objects that satisfy a query may change over time. To address this, we propose a new task, Generalized Reasoning Video Segmentation (GRVS), where the target set depends on query-relevant events accumulated over the observed video history. As new evidence accumulates, the target set can evolve from empty to one or multiple objects, requiring a model to continuously maintain target validity while localizing the valid objects currently in view. To evaluate GRVS, we build Isekai, a diagnostic benchmark of 60 videos and 197 queries across 11 temporal query families, with 12,569 scoring points whose target sets are causally annotated over time. We further propose Sekai, an online framework with two complementary components: an Event Memory Reasoner (EMR) that maintains query-relevant events and evolving target states in structured memory, and a Memory-Grounded Decoder (MGD) that identifies valid targets from this memory and localizes them in the current frame. Experiments show that Sekai significantly outperforms existing online RVS methods, achieving 50.33 J&F and improving over the strongest baseline by 3.2 points. Code, example annotations, and evaluation scripts are available at https://github.com/zenastangbi/Sekai.

</details>

## 8. Dynamical State-Space Diffusion for Spatiotemporal Prediction

**中文标题**: Dynamical State-Space Diffusion for Spatiotemporal Prediction

**作者**: Woojun Lee, Hankyeol Kim, Pilsung Kang

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06652v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06652v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06652v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Diffusion models for temporal prediction typically corrupt each future frame with independent noise, leaving temporal dependence entirely to the denoiser. We propose Dynamical State-Space Diffusion (DSSD), which learns the temporal structure of the corruption process itself. DSSD builds a temporal kernel from the damped oscillatory modes of a stable diagonal state-space model, whose learned decay rates and frequencies let the corruption capture multiple timescales and oscillatory correlations, while the denoiser architecture remains unchanged. Running this model on a finite clip, however, requires the state before the first frame, which is unobserved; starting from a zero state leaves the early frames with less noise than the diffusion schedule prescribes. We avoid this by using the Cholesky factor of the model's stationary covariance as the temporal operator. We prove that this operator is causal, exactly invertible, keeps each frame's noise variance equal to that of standard diffusion, and reduces to the identity at the clean end of the process, and that it is exactly the Kalman innovations form of the state-space model. The denoiser is trained to predict the temporally correlated noise, and sampling removes and reapplies the temporal mixing at each step. On scientific-field and robot-video datasets, DSSD outperforms diffusion baselines that share its denoiser, and the evolution of its learned modes during training shows that the temporal operator adapts to each dataset.

</details>

## 9. Human Motion Prediction via Continual Prior Compensation

**中文标题**: Human Motion Prediction via Continual Prior Compensation

**作者**: Jianwei Tang, Jian-Fang Hu, Tianming Liang, Xiaotong Lin, Jiangxin Sun et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06637v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06637v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06637v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.AI

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Human Motion Prediction (HMP) aims to predict future human poses at different moments according to observed past motion sequences. Previous approaches mainly treated the prediction of different temporal moments as a single prediction task and learned the predictions of varied moments simultaneously, which would encounter a main limitation: the learning of short-term predictions (referring to ``near-future'' prediction) could be hindered by the predictions of long-term (referring to ``far-future'' prediction) motions. In this paper, we develop a novel temporal continual learning framework called Continual Prior Compensation (CPC) to progressively train HMP models, in which we divide the prediction task of motions corresponding to varied temporal moments into several subtasks and train the model in a multi-stage manner. To mitigate the prior information forgetting in the progressive training, we further introduce a learnable random variable Prior Compensation Factor (PCF) to explicitly measure the prior knowledge loss. We theoretically show that the PCF can be efficiently learned together with the model parameters by minimizing a reasonable upper bound of the objective function. The proposed CPC is further enhanced to estimate the prior information loss for each subtask and a new framework called Continual Prior Compensation++ (CPC++) with Fine-Grained Prior Compensation Factor (FGPCF) is finally developed. Our CPC and CPC++ frameworks are quite flexible and can be easily integrated with different HMP backbone models and adapted to various datasets and applications. Extensive experiments on three HMP benchmark datasets using multiple SOTA HMP backbones (PGBIG, siMLPe, MotionMixer, and LTD) demonstrate the effectiveness and flexibility of our frameworks.

</details>

## 10. Learning to Track Cells from Sparse Annotations in 3D Time-Lapse Microscopy

**中文标题**: Learning to Track Cells from Sparse Annotations in 3D Time-Lapse Microscopy

**作者**: Yusuke Uchida

**发布日期**: 2026-11-04

**arXiv ID**: [2611.06624v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06624v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06624v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Reconstructing cell lineages from 3D time-lapse microscopy requires distinguishing cell motion from division and inferring temporally consistent connections. When only a subset of cells is annotated, however, training procedures that treat unannotated regions as background or incorrect correspondences can be problematic. We propose a cell tracking pipeline that integrates detection from sparse annotations, calibration of candidate connections using image features, and joint selection of lineage graph events. For detection, we combine the outputs of 2.5D and 3D models trained to suppress erroneous background supervision at unannotated cells. We then calibrate ordinary link and division probabilities using motion features from dense flow, correspondence features from a learned cell-matching model, and features from models that identify cells immediately before division, dividing parents, and daughters immediately after division. Ordinary links, divisions represented as daughter pairs, and node inclusion are selected jointly under a surrogate objective derived from the evaluation metric, followed by gap filling and temporal coordinate refinement. The pipeline achieves an overall score of 0.9762 in 5-fold cross-validation on 199 videos from 2 zebrafish embryos. With candidates and calibrated values held fixed, joint selection outperforms a sequential method that allows replacement of existing divisions and reoptimization of ordinary links by 0.0105. The improvement arises primarily from division recovery.

</details>
