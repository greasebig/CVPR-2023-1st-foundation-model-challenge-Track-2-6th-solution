## 1. Faster, Smaller, and Better-Looking: Fast-GSVC for 2D Gaussian Video Coding

**中文标题**: Faster, Smaller, and Better-Looking: Fast-GSVC for 2D Gaussian Video Coding

**作者**: Longan Wang, Yuang Shi, Chia Qian Tan, Wei Tsang Ooi

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.30629v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30629v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30629v1)

**项目主页**: https://longanwang-cs.github.io/fastgsvc/.

**类别**: cs.MM

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

2D Gaussians have emerged as a promising explicit representation for videos. However, fitting Gaussians to a video sequence requires substantial computation, resulting in long encoding times. In this work, we present Fast-GSVC, a 2D Gaussian video codec that accelerates encoding while improving rate-distortion performance. We identify the key bottlenecks in 2DGS training and accelerate optimization through improved initialization, smoother yet more aggressive parameter updates, coarse-to-fine progressive Gaussian optimization, and optical-flow guidance for both Gaussian initialization and temporal propagation. We also discovered that the quantization fine-tuning process of the existing codec does not fully exploit temporal coherence between frames, leading to suboptimal compression. To address this, we preserve spatiotemporal correlations to maintain concentrated residuals and introduce temporal-aware quantization for further improved rate-distortion performance. In our experiments on the UVG dataset, Fast-GSVC reduces encoding time by up to 92.5%, corresponding to a 13.4X speedup over GSVC. At comparable PSNR, it reduces file size by up to 58%; at comparable bit-per-pixel, it improves PSNR by up to 2.5 dB. Project page: https://longanwang-cs.github.io/fastgsvc/.

</details>

## 2. RAPID: Codec-Aware Video Decoding for Efficient Long-Video Understanding

**中文标题**: RAPID: Codec-Aware Video Decoding for Efficient Long-Video Understanding

**作者**: Yuna Lee, Yulhwa Kim

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.30557v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30557v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30557v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.MM

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Recent vision-language models (VLMs) can process hour-long videos, but the large number of input frames incurs substantial inference overhead. Existing approaches to efficient long-video understanding largely overlook video decoding, which can take longer than LLM prefill. We propose RAPID (Reference- and Access-Point-Informed Decoding), a codec-aware framework that exploits information embedded in compressed bitstreams to reduce both frame decoding and selection costs. RAPID consists of two components. RAPID-Dec exploits reference-frame information and random access points to skip unnecessary frames and decode the retained frames in parallel while preserving exact reconstruction of the target frames. It achieves up to a 3.7x decoding speedup over the fastest baseline on H.264/AVC videos. RAPID-Sel uses keyframes as coarse representatives of video segments, avoiding the dense candidate decoding and scoring required by existing frame selection methods. It achieves comparable accuracy with up to a 3.0x preprocessing speedup. Together, RAPID achieves a 4.9x time to first token (TTFT) speedup on hour-long videos over a state-of-the-art frame selection method using the fastest baseline decoder.

</details>

## 3. Wrist-Camera Video Diffusion as Generalizable Robot Policies

**中文标题**: Wrist-Camera Video Diffusion as Generalizable Robot Policies

**作者**: Jingyuan Liu, Yongwei Chen, Yishan Yang, Zexin He, Xingang Pan et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.31079v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31079v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31079v1)

**项目主页**: https://wrist-video-policy.github.io.

**类别**: cs.RO, cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

A generalist manipulation policy should perform new tasks, operate in unfamiliar scenes, and transfer skills across robot embodiments. We present Wrist Video Policy (WVP), a closed-loop manipulation framework that learns how manipulation unfolds through video prediction and recovers executable motion through geometric estimation. Our key idea is to represent robot motion through wrist-camera trajectories, which determine the corresponding end-effector poses through a fixed hand-eye transformation. We pair a video generator that predicts future wrist views with a pose estimator adapted from a pretrained 3D model to recover the corresponding camera trajectories. Model stitching maps the generator's predicted latents directly into the estimator's intermediate feature space, bypassing RGB decoding. This decomposition allows task knowledge to expand through broad human and robot video data while reusing geometric motion estimation across tasks and embodiments. The resulting pose estimator transfers across Franka Panda, PiPER-X, and Galaxea R1 Pro without further fine-tuning. Across tabletop and laundry-room settings, WVP generalizes to tasks ranging from precise grasping and inserting to handling deformable objects, achieving a success rate of over 50% in zero-shot out-of-distribution evaluations. Our project page is at https://wrist-video-policy.github.io.

</details>

## 4. SurgeCount: A Protocol-Guided Composition Harness for Bimanual Surgical Sponge Counting

**中文标题**: SurgeCount: A Protocol-Guided Composition Harness for Bimanual Surgical Sponge Counting

**作者**: Sung Eun Kim, Xiaoman Zhang, Xucheng Wang, Pranav Rajpurkar

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.31037v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31037v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31037v1)

**类别**: cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

We present SurgeCount, a system for auditable robotic surgical sponge counting that composes learned skills under a written procedural protocol. Retained sponges cause preventable harm, and surgical teams count every sponge before closure to reduce that risk. The robot repeatedly isolates one deformable sponge from a tangled pile and places it in the next numbered counting-tray slot, requiring handling of occluded grasp threads while preserving step order. We train one subtask-conditioned policy on eleven short skills and learn when each skill ends. At each learned boundary, a stateful vision-language model selects the next skill from an operator-authored specification of step order, visual verification, and recovery. This creates temporal commitment to each subtask and enables failures to be recovered by recomposing existing skills rather than using a dedicated recovery policy. We evaluate five methods across 100 real-robot trials using 20 matched layouts. SurgeCount fills 47 of 60 ordered slots and completes 12 of 20 procedures, compared with 27 of 60 and 3 of 20 for the strongest of four end-to-end policies trained on the same data. Replacing the detailed protocol prompt with a minimal prompt reduces performance to 29 of 60 slots and 5 of 20 completed procedures. These results show that long-horizon performance depends not only on visuomotor capacity, but also on how learned skills are committed, verified, and composed. This tabletop proof of concept motivates further evaluation of protocol-guided robotic sponge counting in clinically representative settings.

</details>

## 5. World Models as Native Latent Action Models

**中文标题**: World Models as Native Latent Action Models

**作者**: Qinyue Li, Weijie Qiao, Qingyi Tao, Zerui Gong, Zhe Yang et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.30898v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30898v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30898v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.LG, cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Large and diverse video sources, such as egocentric human video and heterogeneous robot recordings, carry rich dynamics but come either without actions or without a shared action space. Latent action models (LAMs) can recover these dynamics, but they train their inverse- and forward-dynamics models (IDM, FDM) from scratch, trading off pixel reconstruction against latent capacity and accuracy. Pretrained video world models natively generate high-fidelity dynamics, yet represent them densely, entangled across all layers. Viewing the world model as a LAM, we show that these dynamics can be extracted with minimal changes to the model. Native LAM operates one pretrained video diffusion transformer as both IDM and FDM, with learnable dynamics registers as the only causal bridge between the two, and trains on counterfactual temporal pairs that differ in motion alone, so that the registers distill a video's dynamics into a compact native latent action. We further propose a pretraining paradigm for vision-language-action (VLA) policies, Pretraining Actions from World models (PAWlicy), in which Native LAM serves as a dynamics teacher on large-scale video; unlike world action models, it never generates video. Native latent actions outperform existing LAMs on the LARY benchmark in both action classification and control regression, and replay the same motion on new robots, in new scenes, and from human hands to robots, although no transfer objective is used in training. With a 3.8B model, PAWlicy reaches a 93.6% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 trained with domain-randomized demonstrations, the highest among VLAs. On RoboDojo, it is competitive on every dimension we evaluate, and its Precision, evaluated by us, is on par with the leading leaderboard entries. On real robots, it improves over the pi_0.5 baseline on both tasks. Both stages use only public data and a publicly released world model.

</details>

## 6. Place-Revisit Consistency Evaluation of Reference Poses

**中文标题**: Place-Revisit Consistency Evaluation of Reference Poses

**作者**: Jianzhu Huai, Yujia Zhang, Grzegorz Jozkow

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.30741v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30741v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30741v1)

**类别**: cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Reference trajectories are widely used to evaluate odometry and simultaneous localization and mapping systems. However, local inconsistencies in benchmark reference poses can bias such evaluations. This paper presents a place-revisit consistency evaluation method for reference poses in mobile sensing datasets. When a platform revisits a previously observed place after a sufficient temporal gap, the relative pose implied by the reference trajectory should agree with relative poses estimated directly from onboard sensors. We instantiate this idea with lidar scan registration and stereo-image pose estimation, and apply it to the KITTI Odometry and SNAIL Radar. The KITTI analysis identifies several revisit regions where the public reference poses disagree with sensor-derived relative poses, with median translation discrepancies up to 6.60 m. Representative point-cloud overlays further localize these discrepancies and support the use of place revisits as a benchmark-validation tool.

</details>

## 7. Coordinating Perception and Planning for Test-Time Adaptation in End-to-End Driving under Distribution Shifts

**中文标题**: Coordinating Perception and Planning for Test-Time Adaptation in End-to-End Driving under Distribution Shifts

**作者**: Shiyu Fang, Yiming Cui, Qihang Huang, Zhan Chen, Collister Chua et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.30739v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30739v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30739v1)

**类别**: cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

End-to-end autonomous driving has achieved significant milestones, yet its reliance on the i.i.d. assumption renders it vulnerable to real-world distribution shifts. While Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) offers a promising paradigm for on-the-fly enhancement, its application to driving is hindered by two barriers: (i) multi-task adaptation dependency, where updates to upstream perception also affect downstream planning, while perception confidence alone does not directly assess these downstream effects; and (ii) continuous adaptation instability, where recursive online updates over long horizons cause compounding parameter drift. To address these challenges, we propose P2-TTA, a plug-and-play TTA framework for the continual adaptation of end-to-end driving models. P2-TTA coordinates adaptation through a joint objective combining perception entropy minimization with a retrospective temporal planning consistency objective derived from observed ego motion. To stabilize online updates, we use an EMA teacher as a moving parameter reference and reject candidate updates that increase historical planning loss. Extensive experiments across multiple models demonstrate that, by updating an average of 0.11 per mille of parameters with an average adaptation time of 19.9 ms on different backbones, P2-TTA achieves average recovery rates of 36.4%, 30.0%, and 9.3% for L2, collision rate, and PDMS, respectively, under severe distribution shifts. Under a shared perception-planning objective, P2-TTA achieves the highest L2 and collision recovery among the compared TTA baselines on UniAD.

</details>

## 8. VeriTouch: Verified Visuo-Tactile World Models that Improve Robot Policy Learning

**中文标题**: VeriTouch: Verified Visuo-Tactile World Models that Improve Robot Policy Learning

**作者**: Shuxun Wang, Xiaonan Jiang, Zhaoyu Wang, Ziyuan Chu, Xihang Yue et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.30572v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30572v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30572v1)

**类别**: cs.RO, cs.AI

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Scaling visuo-tactile policy learning is constrained by the high cost of real-world data collection and the difficulty of building realistic simulations. Generative models offer a scalable source of training data, yet useful demonstrations require plausible tactile feedback, temporal alignment with visual contact, and accurate actions. To this end, we present VeriTouch, a framework for generating and verifying synthetic visuo-tactile trajectories for policy learning. Conditioned on initial images and task instructions, our world model, VeriGen, jointly generates high-fidelity dual-view videos and realistic tactile sequences over complete manipulation trajectories. By integrating a dedicated tactile expert with a pretrained video model, it captures realistic visual details and tactile deformation dynamics through joint visuo-tactile modeling. Our multimodal verifier VeriEval then checks visual quality while focusing on tactile reliability, assessing tactile realism, contact-force plausibility, and temporal alignment between visible contact and tactile signals, thereby directly filtering out unreliable sequences. Finally, our inverse dynamics model, VeriIDM, combines global scene information from both camera views with fine-grained gripper details and motion across neighboring frames to extract actions from verified videos. Experiments on five real-world tasks show that synthetic data improves all four evaluated policies, with further scaling synthetic trajectories substantially boosting policy performance without additional real-world data collection.

</details>

## 9. SOAR: temporal Structure-guided One-step Action generation for Robotic manipulation

**中文标题**: SOAR: temporal Structure-guided One-step Action generation for Robotic manipulation

**作者**: Di Xin, Mingfei Han, Genming Cui, Yuanxiang Chen, Xiaolong Wu et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.30363v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30363v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30363v1)

**类别**: cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Efficient robotic manipulation requires temporally coordinated actions with low inference latency. Multi-step diffusion and flow policies progressively refine action trajectories but require costly iterative inference, whereas one-step policies eliminate iterative trajectory refinement to reduce inference cost. We introduce **SOAR**, a structure-guided one-step generative policy that improves action quality while preserving one-step efficiency. SOAR predicts an observation-conditioned discrete cosine transform (DCT) representation of future actions, capturing temporal variations across multiple frequency bands to provide explicit guidance from key temporal structural information. Unlike velocity-parameterized one-step flow policies that recover clean actions indirectly from a predicted velocity field, SOAR directly generates clean action trajectories in action space. This aligns the model output with the executable control target and provides a direct interface for structural calibration. The predicted structure further corrects the generated trajectory according to their spectral discrepancy, without requiring an additional evaluation of the generative backbone. During training, the corresponding MeanFlow velocity is derived from the calibrated clean action and used for consistency learning, while an endpoint objective aligns training with the pure-noise condition used for one-step inference. Across 53 Adroit and Meta-World tasks, SOAR achieves 83.9% overall success with only 8.7 ms inference latency. When applied to the VLA model, π0.5, SOAR further achieves 97.2% on LIBERO and 71.7% on RoboTwin 2.0. Real-world experiments and extensive ablations further validate its effectiveness and efficiency.

</details>

## 10. STAR: Structure-Aware Temporal Action Recovery for Point Supervised Temporal Action Localization

**中文标题**: STAR: Structure-Aware Temporal Action Recovery for Point Supervised Temporal Action Localization

**作者**: Yawar Rehman, Min Young Kim

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

**arXiv ID**: [2611.30354v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30354v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30354v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Point supervised temporal action localization is becoming the need of time in this era of ever growing and non-stop video data. Existing method mainly rely on snippet level action response to generate pseudo labels. However, these labels often suffer from boundary ambiguity, over extended proposals, and weak temporal structure. To address these issues, we propose STAR, a structure-aware Temporal Action Recovery framework for point supervised temporal action localization. STAR works by enhancing the base S model with temporal difference interaction with attention module to capture forward and backward snippet changes, and introduces the learning of start, core, and end roles to model the internal temporal structure of actions. In addition, STAR further improves the pseudo label generation through All point proposal ranking and Rolebank based structural re-ranking, which suppress neighbor crossing and structurally inconsistent proposals. Finally, temporal action detector to STAR feedback supervision transfers high quality detector prediction back to the STAR model through soft start, core, end, action, and background targets. This helps to further refine the pseudo labels. Extensive experiments on four standard benchmarks shows the effectiveness of STAR. By using I3D features, STAR achieves 67.2% at AVG@0.1:0.7 on THUMOS'14, 58.1% on GTEA, 74.6% on BEOID, and 31.7% at AVG@0.5:0.95 on ActivityNet-1.3, outperforming previous point supervised methods across multiple benchmarks.

## 31. Damped Atomic Wave Integration Neurons (DAWIN): A Universal Primitive for Continuous-Time Spatiotemporal Learning

**作者**: Byunggu Yu, Justin Kim

**发布日期**: 2026-11-23

<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Conventional artificial neurons separate spatial feature aggregation from continuous physical time, limiting their ability to model irregular observations. **作者**: Xin Zhou, Cong Miao

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Causal video world models generate block by block from their own previous outputs. Small errors in early blocks are fed back as history and grow until the scene and the robot fall apart. Teacher forcing trains on clean ground-truth history, so the model never sees the history it will actually condition on, and it also finds a shortcut: copying the previous frame instead of predicting how the scene changes. We present Dynamics Forcing, a training scheme that keeps real video as the only supervision and reshapes how history is presented. Dynamics Forcing needs no teacher model, no distillation, and no additional model at inference. Applied to a 5B video model with LoRA on 77 hours of manipulation video on a single GPU, it better matches unseen real continuations and reduces error accumulation.

<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generate sequences of actions from visual observations, proprioceptive states, and language instructions. In embodied interaction, executing these actions influences subsequent observations and robot states, providing a natural predictive signal for policy learning. We introduce JEPA-Tuner, a predictive fine-tuning framework that regularizes the VLA's native policy guidance through action-conditioned future prediction. Experiments on LIBERO and Meta-World show that JEPA-Tuner improves task success under synchronous inference with better multi-modal guidance embeddings. For asynchronous control, JEPA-Tuner improves performance and smoothness across different latency settings on both LIBERO simulator and multi-size real robot demonstrations.
