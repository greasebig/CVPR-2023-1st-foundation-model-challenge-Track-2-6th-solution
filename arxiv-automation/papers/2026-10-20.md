## 1. VideoMaMa++: Temporally Consistent Video Matting via Preserve-and-Refine Token

**中文标题**: VideoMaMa++: Temporally Consistent Video Matting via Preserve-and-Refine Token

**作者**: Sangbeom Lim, Seoung Wug Oh, Heeji Yoon, Seungryong Kim, Joon-Young Lee

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26857v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26857v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26857v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Diffusion-based video matting has recently emerged as a leading approach, leveraging video diffusion priors from internet-scale pretraining to achieve strong zero-shot generalization from limited synthetic data. However, the heavy compute of these diffusion backbones limits inference to a short chunk of just 6 to 24 frames, so even ordinary videos span multiple chunks. Because each chunk is matted independently from coarse mask guidance, existing methods fail to maintain consistency across chunk boundaries, producing flickering edges and discontinuous transparency. We address this with a mask-guided diffusion-matting framework built on two ideas. The RGB and mask conditioning are represented as separate token streams that interact through full spatio-temporal self-attention, letting the model adaptively combine fine RGB texture with coarse mask layout rather than committing to a fixed channel-wise fusion. On top of this, we propose a novel pair of learnable embeddings, the preserve token and the refine token, that act as a per-frame conditioning interface and enable a sliding-window inference scheme in which already-generated mattes are propagated across chunk boundaries for local temporal consistency, complemented by a globally shared reference anchor at every chunk to prevent error accumulation in long sequences without any additional training. Across standard video matting benchmarks and in-the-wild long footage, our framework outperforms prior diffusion-based methods in both fine-detail accuracy and temporal consistency, and uniquely maintains consistency across chunk boundaries on long videos.

</details>

## 2. Manga2Animate: An Agentic Framework for Faithful Manga Animation with Script and Storyboard Planning

**中文标题**: Manga2Animate: An Agentic Framework for Faithful Manga Animation with Script and Storyboard Planning

**作者**: Sheng-Wei Zhang, Hsi-An Chen, Yan-Ting Liou, Chih-Hsuan Chen, Shih-Chen Tseng et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26851v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26851v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26851v1)

**项目主页**: https://annchenn.github.io/Manga2Animate/

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Adapting existing manga into coherent animation that stays faithful to the source is a valuable yet challenging task. Existing methods either access the manga mainly through text, losing its characters and art style, or feed manga pages directly into video generation models, which misinterpret the manga's structure and produce incorrect storylines. In this paper, we propose Manga2Animate, a multi-agent framework that grounds every stage of generation in the source manga. We decompose the task into two intermediate representations, an animation script and a visual storyboard. Each representation is produced by a creator agent and reviewed against the source panels by a paired reviewer agent. We tailor representation-specific selection and dual-level feedback to progressively refine both before synthesizing the final animation. Extensive experiments and a user study demonstrate that Manga2Animate produces animations more faithful to the source manga and more consistent across shots than state-of-the-art approaches. Project page: https://annchenn.github.io/Manga2Animate/

</details>

## 3. SpecKCast: Spectral Kalman Forecasting for Diffusion Acceleration

**中文标题**: SpecKCast: Spectral Kalman Forecasting for Diffusion Acceleration

**作者**: Javad Rajabi, Hakki Can Karaimer, David B. Lindell, Babak Taati, Michael S. Brown

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26837v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26837v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26837v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) achieve high-fidelity image and video generation, but they suffer from substantial inference costs due to repeated transformer evaluations across many denoising steps. Feature caching reduces this cost by reusing features across denoising steps. Recent forecasting methods extend this idea by extrapolating features to skipped steps instead of reusing them. However, existing forecasting methods extrapolate the features they predict uniformly, even though the spatial-frequency components of these features change most at different stages of denoising. They also overlook that denoising progresses nonuniformly, so equal step intervals can correspond to very different amounts of change. These mismatches amplify forecast errors as more steps are skipped, limiting the achievable acceleration. We propose SpecKCast, a training-free method that forecasts the model output in the frequency domain. SpecKCast applies a 2D discrete cosine transform (DCT) to the output and tracks each coefficient with a lightweight Kalman filter along a band-specific time coordinate derived from the noise schedule. The filter tracks each coefficient's value and rate of change, and the transformer is evaluated only when the filter's forecast uncertainty exceeds a threshold. Experiments on FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image, Wan2.1-14B, and HunyuanVideo show that SpecKCast achieves a better trade-off between acceleration and fidelity than existing caching and forecasting methods, reaching up to 6x speedup with less than 0.05 GiB of additional memory.

</details>

## 4. Real-Time Radar Semantic and Panoptic Scene Understanding

**中文标题**: Real-Time Radar Semantic and Panoptic Scene Understanding

**作者**: Santosh Parit, Julia Hindel, Rohit Mohan, Georgi Pramatarov, Paul Newman et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26816v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26816v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26816v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Robust scene understanding under varying environmental conditions is essential for safe autonomous driving. While cameras and LiDAR degrade in adverse weather and poor lighting, Frequency-Modulated Continuous-Wave (FMCW) spinning radar provides dependable 360° perception at extended ranges with minimal compute cost. However, real-time FMCW radar scene understanding is challenging due to complex signal returns, limited temporal resolution, and scarce annotated datasets. We introduce StreamRPS, a novel framework for streaming FMCW radar panoptic segmentation, comprising an automated label-generation pipeline via LiDAR scene completion and a real-time streaming network for semantic and panoptic segmentation. Our encoder-decoder streaming architecture comprises a radar-specific hierarchical Transformer encoder and task-specific decoders for semantic or panoptic prediction. Our encoder introduces a stem to capture range-dependent signatures, anisotropic patch embeddings to preserve angular resolution, and axial polar attention to separately model the periodic azimuth and bounded range axes. We further introduce azimuth roll rotation augmentation to simulate ego-vehicle rotation during training. Comprehensive experiments on the SAX and BOREAS datasets validate both our auto-labeling methodology and streaming segmentation network against established baselines. We outperform the strongest baseline by at least 1.4pp while processing sectors at over 400 FPS in a 4-sector streaming setting. We additionally provide manual annotations for 100 SAX radar scans, enabling independent evaluation of the entire framework.

</details>

## 5. PICA-WAM: Visual Perturbation Invariance via Cross-Appearance Self-Alignment in World Action Models

**中文标题**: PICA-WAM: Visual Perturbation Invariance via Cross-Appearance Self-Alignment in World Action Models

**作者**: Yude Zou, Yifei Yao, Guanbo Jiao, Jianming Ma, Guanjie Zheng et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26813v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26813v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26813v1)

**代码仓库**: https://github.com/YudeZou/PICA-WAM-release

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

World Action Models (WAMs) jointly predict future video and generate actions, yet remain sensitive to task-irrelevant visual perturbations, i.e., changes in visual appearance that should not affect robot behavior. We identify a gap in conventional WAM training: video prediction encourages representations to preserve appearance information, while action generation does not explicitly enforce invariance to task-irrelevant appearance. Data augmentation exposes WAMs to such perturbations but does not require perturbed and nominal observations to produce consistent action representations, and alignment with external pretrained features does not guarantee the desired invariance. To address this gap, we propose PICA-WAM, a self-supervised training framework that casts perturbation invariance as an auxiliary cross-appearance self-alignment task for WAMs. An exponential-moving-average (EMA) teacher processes the nominal observation to provide a deeper-layer target, while the online WAM processes a perturbed observation and aligns its shallower-layer action representation to this target through a lightweight projection head. Across four strong WAMs evaluated on RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO-Plus, EBench, and a real robot, PICA-WAM consistently improves robustness under visual perturbations, preserves nominal performance in simulation, and raises the real robot success rate. PICA-WAM further reduces the sensitivity of video features to task-irrelevant appearance and its propagation into actions, as shown by paired layer-wise diagnostics. Code: https://github.com/YudeZou/PICA-WAM-release

</details>

## 6. State World Models: Execute the Rules, Generate the Pixels

**中文标题**: State World Models: Execute the Rules, Generate the Pixels

**作者**: Chan Yan Ki, Yanran Zhang, Wenzhao Zheng, Xiaofeng Wang, Zheng Zhu et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26811v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26811v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26811v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Existing world models can generate action-conditioned video from a single image. Many focus on camera movement, allowing users to explore the generated scene. However, navigation alone does not ensure that an action follows persistent rules when its outcome depends on state that is invisible in the current frame. We present Stateful Harness, a framework that separates world evolution from video rendering to support stateful and rule-governed interaction. It maintains an evolving world state under specified rules, renders each resulting event with a frozen video model, and uses the generated clip to update the world state for the next turn. At each turn, the harness resolves the action selected by the user and compiles the resulting event into an instruction, while a preview clip from an editable 3D scene provides spatial and camera guidance. To evaluate rule compliance, we build WorldState-Bench, a benchmark of 50 counterfactual twin pairs that share an initial frame and action but differ in hidden state and required outcome. Under rule-only prompting, the best of the 11 evaluated video backends produces the required endings for both cases in 32% of pairs. For MiniMax-H3, specifying the required endings raises this rate from 32% to 64%, while the full harness reaches 92% with the same frozen model. These results support separating rule execution from video rendering for world interactions with hidden state.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Recent advances in text-to-video generation have enabled high-fidelity single-shot synthesis, yet practical storytelling creation requires multi-shot videos with both strong inter-shot consistency and fine-grained controllability. Existing multi-shot video generation approaches achieve strong consistency but still fall short in controllability. In this paper, we propose Mise-en-Video (MeV), a highly controllable multi-shot video generation framework that allows video creators to conduct staging the multi-shot in the 3D space and then generate the corresponding multi-shot video. To achieve this, our framework combines a hierarchical attention mechanism for multi-level narrative consistency with 3D-aware layout guidance for precise, per-shot spatial control. To support training and evaluation, we develop standardized annotation pipelines and construct a 160K-video dataset with hierarchical multi-shot texts and oriented 3D bounding boxes. Extensive experiments demonstrate that MeV consistently outperforms strong baselines in video quality, inter-shot consistency, and subject-level controllability.

## 8. V-JEPA Hand: Simple, Accurate and Fast 3D Hand and Camera Trajectory Recovery from Egocentric Videos

**中文标题**: V-JEPA Hand: Simple, Accurate and Fast 3D Hand and Camera Trajectory Recovery from Egocentric Videos

**作者**: Ruicheng Li, Qixiu Li, Yu Deng, Sicheng Xu, Jiaolong Yang et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26780v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26780v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26780v1)

**项目主页**: https://qft-333.github.io/V-JEPA-Hand-Page/.

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Egocentric video supports diverse applications, including robot learning, augmented reality, and human activity understanding. Recovering metric 3D hand trajectories from such video requires estimating both hand motion and camera egomotion to express the hands in a consistent reference frame. Many existing pipelines recover hand and camera motion in separate stages, combining hand detection, hand reconstruction, camera tracking, and trajectory refinement. We present V-JEPA Hand, a simple feed-forward video model that jointly reconstructs bilateral hand motion and camera motion from egocentric clips. It uses a self-supervised V-JEPA 2.1 encoder to extract spatio-temporal features from full-frame video and a query-based predictor to decode these features into per-frame hand poses, hand presence, and camera motion. We use the predicted camera motion to express the hand trajectories in a common world frame. The model accepts known focal lengths or predicts them when unavailable. It reconstructs each clip in a single forward pass. We train both components end to end in a single stage on a mixture of diverse pseudo-labelled videos and high-precision 3D annotations. A single model achieves the lowest coverage-aware mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) among comparable baselines on ARCTIC, HOT3D, and EgoStandard. It also yields lower world-frame trajectory errors than the compared SLAM-based pipelines. Ablations support the benefits of video pretraining, temporal context, and mixed supervision. V-JEPA Hand achieves a forward-pass throughput of 93.4 FPS on a single RTX A6000. Project page: https://qft-333.github.io/V-JEPA-Hand-Page/.

</details>

## 9. PVBench: Quantifying Physical Plausibility in Video Generation with Object-Level Physical Measurements

**中文标题**: PVBench: Quantifying Physical Plausibility in Video Generation with Object-Level Physical Measurements

**作者**: Kaiwei Chen, Moran Li, Zhenye Gan, Yabiao Wang, Chengjie Wang et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26711v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26711v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26711v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Recent video generation models produce visually compelling outputs, yet their physical plausibility remains poorly measured. Physical plausibility asks whether generated objects obey geometry, kinematics, dynamics, and conservation laws. Existing benchmarks rely on coarse visual-language model judgments or target embodied world models rather than video generation itself. We introduce PVBench, a physical video benchmark that quantitatively measures the plausibility of generated videos across 46 dimensions organized into seven domains, spanning: i) instruction and semantic fidelity, ii) behavioral and causal consistency, iii) perceptual and temporal quality, iv) geometric and spatial consistency, v) material properties and field states, vi) kinematic and spatiotemporal persistence, vii) and mechanical plausibility with conservation laws. PVBench comprises 600 carefully crafted prompts, of which 30% involve physical embodied agents such as robot arms, humanoids, quadrupeds, and multi-agent systems. For challenging physically tractable dimensions, it pairs each with specialized vision models that derive verifiable, physically grounded measurements, such as object-level 6D pose, metric scale, surface normals, kinematics, and numerical conservation-law residuals. It thus avoids relying solely on subjective scoring, while supporting both text-to-video and image-to-video generation. Finally, we validate the agreement between automatic metrics and human judgments through human evaluation. Meanwhile, we evaluate mainstream open-source and closed-source models and present several insights. By releasing the benchmark, prompts, and calibration protocol, we aim to foster physically grounded video generation.

</details>

## 10. From Short Clips to Real-Time Streams: Identity-Aware Supervision and Context-Efficient Generation for Video Face Swapping

**中文标题**: From Short Clips to Real-Time Streams: Identity-Aware Supervision and Context-Efficient Generation for Video Face Swapping

**作者**: Zekai Luo, Yuwei Liang, Ruiqi Ma, Haoyu Huang, Xuehao Wang et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-19

**arXiv ID**: [2610.26681v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26681v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26681v1)

**项目主页**: https://aim-uofa.github.io/StreamingSwap/

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Video face swapping transfers the reference identity to a target video while preserving its other attributes and motion. Despite recent progress in reference-guided flow-based methods, jointly achieving faithful identity transfer, high fidelity, and real-time generation over long video streams remains challenging. To address this challenge, we rethink the task-specific structure of reference-guided video face swapping. First, face swapping changes only identity while preserving most visual content, yet flow-matching supervision is weakly sensitive to these changes. We therefore develop Joint Rec-ID Loss, which balances explicit identity supervision and latent reconstruction according to the noise level. Second, the target video already provides non-edited attributes and temporal dynamics, enabling generation with fewer spatial tokens and shorter temporal contexts through Face-RoI Generation and Short-Context Rollout Training. By integrating these designs, we propose StreamingSwap, a reference-guided flow-matching framework for real-time streaming video face swapping. We also introduce HumanFaceVid, comprising 1,000 hours of cinematic training data and evaluation sets of 200 short and 200 long videos. Experiments demonstrate that our method achieves competitive identity transfer and visual quality at 20 FPS on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, with stable generation over videos up to 10 minutes long. Project webpage: https://aim-uofa.github.io/StreamingSwap/

</details>

## 111. Action-Aligned Latent Planning for Versatile Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

**中文标题**: Action-Aligned Latent Planning for Versatile Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

**作者**: Yutang Lin, Guanya Shi, C. Karen Liu

**发布日期**: 2026-10-17

**arXiv ID**: [2610.24675v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24675v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24675v1)

**类别**: cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Learning a single perceptive policy for diverse humanoid loco-manipulation requires unifying high-level task decisions without losing the state-dependent physical responses that make each interaction succeed. This makes the representation between high-level planning and whole-body control critical: kinematic or contact targets provide compact abstractions but do not directly encode interaction-specific control responses, while directly predicting low-level actions requires the planner to model high-frequency closed-loop feedback. We propose FIBER, a framework that uses the executable latent space of a frozen behavioral foundation model (BFM) as a shared planning representation for heterogeneous interaction skills. Our latent policy projection (LPP) maps independently acquired task-specific policies into this representation by identifying latent commands whose induced actions match their local control responses, enabling lower-rate planning while retaining the BFM's high-frequency state feedback. After projection, we aggregate closed-loop latent rollouts from all skills and train a single perceptive flow planner entirely offline. We further introduce AH-BFM, an adaptive-horizon BFM trained with multi-gamma forward-backward (FB) learning to support both precise control and temporally extended whole-body behavior. We evaluate FIBER on box pickup, door opening, and object grasping in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid. Across 128 diverse variations per task in simulation, FIBER achieves 42.0% mean success, more than tripling projected latent replay (11.6%) and substantially outperforming kinematic planning (3.5%). Real-world experiments further demonstrate sim-to-real transfer, skill chaining, and conditional steering through the same unified planner.

## 112. LIBERO-Dynamic: Benchmarking Robotic Manipulation in Dynamic Environments

**中文标题**: LIBERO-Dynamic: Benchmarking Robotic Manipulation in Dynamic Environments

**作者**: Shaojie Ren, Jingwen Sun, Zezhi Liu, Hongjin Chen, Xin Jin et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-17

**arXiv ID**: [2610.24579v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24579v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24579v1)

**类别**: cs.RO

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Most simulation benchmarks for robotic manipulation keep target objects static and treat policy inference as instantaneous. These assumptions simplify evaluation but hide how a moving target evolves during computation and how stale an observation is when an action is executed. We introduce LIBERO-Dynamic, a dynamic extension of all 40 LIBERO tasks. It drives objects through contact friction along three trajectory families and speed tiers, while advancing the physical world according to calibrated, model-specific inference latency. The same mechanism supports training by replaying verified static demonstrations and compensating robot actions online for target displacement, producing 9,000 quality-filtered dynamic trajectories. We evaluate six pretrained policies on 1,800 dynamic conditions per configuration, compare execution strategies and action horizons with fixed checkpoints, and fine-tune three architectures using dynamic and mixed data. Faster motion degrades every policy but not uniformly; execution timing and horizon can change the behavior of the same policy; and asynchronous replanning benefits from frequent feedback only when the action queue avoids underflow. Dynamic demonstrations improve each architecture under at least one recipe, with gains varying by temporal conditioning, data composition, and training budget. A small-scale physical study shows task-dependent execution-strategy differences consistent with simulation. LIBERO-Dynamic makes object motion, inference latency, and action execution explicit and controllable for evaluating and improving robot policies in moving-target manipulation.

</details>
