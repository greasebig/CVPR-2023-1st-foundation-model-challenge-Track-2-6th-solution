## 1. A Better Critic Matters for Distribution Matching Distillation

**中文标题**: A Better Critic Matters for Distribution Matching Distillation

**作者**: Zongze Wu, Xianbang Wang, Zhengyang Geng, Mingdeng Cao, Zhoutong Zhang et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.42135v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42135v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42135v1)

**类别**: cs.CV, cs.LG

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) trains a few-step student using the difference between a teacher's score and an online critic's estimate of the student distribution's score. We investigate what the critic needs, in capacity and in information, to provide a reliable training signal. On the capacity side, controlled experiments across seven SiT model sizes on ImageNet show that increasing teacher size while keeping a small critic fixed can degrade student quality and cause training collapse. Scaling the critic alongside the teacher, a strategy we call joint scaling, prevents collapse in our experiments and allows the student to benefit from larger teachers. On the information side, rather than forcing the critic to model a step-agnostic mixture of student outputs, we introduce a step-aware critic (STAC) that conditions on the student's generation step to estimate step-specific scores. Together, joint scaling and STAC consistently improve few-step distillation across class-conditional ImageNet generation, text-to-image generation with SDXL and FLUX.2, and text-to-video generation with Wan2.1.

</details>

## 2. Think in 360°: Benchmarking and Bootstrapping Panoramic Video Reasoning

**中文标题**: Think in 360°: Benchmarking and Bootstrapping Panoramic Video Reasoning

**作者**: Lidong Lu, Lidong Zou, Xinyi Mao, Jiaxin Lv, Zhenquan Li et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.42082v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42082v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42082v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

A panoramic video captures all directions around the camera, but relevant evidence can appear in different directions and at different times. Understanding it requires models to connect observations across the viewing sphere and time. We introduce PanoVideoBench, the first manually annotated benchmark for comprehensive panoramic video understanding. Its 1,000 questions span five categories and 15 question types, with diagnostic annotations for target search and panoramic capabilities. Evaluation of 11 proprietary, open-source, and panoramic-specialized models identifies cross-view tracking and sphere-time evidence integration as their weakest capabilities. To strengthen these capabilities, we construct PanoVideoCoT, the first reasoning dataset grounded in real panoramic video, through sphere-time-grounded synthesis that converts verified panoramic observations and successful agentic explorations into 13,465 QA-CoT pairs. Starting from a general video MLLM, PanoVideoReasoner learns from this corpus through Panoramic CoT SFT and Evidence-Privileged On-Policy Self-Distillation, a panoramic instantiation of Vision-OPD's OPSD in which the teacher uses verified perspective views to guide student-generated trajectories. It reaches 55.7% on PanoVideoBench, improving the backbone by 12.5 points and the previous SOTA by 2.6. The gains concentrate on the same spatial, cross-view, and temporal capabilities exposed by the benchmark, showing that grounded sphere-time supervision can equip a general video MLLM with panoramic reasoning.

</details>

## 3. Any Forcing: Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability for Autoregressive Video Generation

**中文标题**: Any Forcing: Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability for Autoregressive Video Generation

**作者**: Jiahua Xu, Yijie Fang, Nannan Wang, Xunhao Lin, Li Cao et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.42031v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42031v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42031v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Autoregressive video generation enables efficient inference through streaming prediction. However, existing methods are typically restricted to fixed sampling steps, limiting the flexibility to balance generation quality and efficiency, while one-step generation suffers from degraded visual quality and error accumulation over long sequences, leading to content drift and generation collapse. To address these issues, we propose Any Forcing, a unified framework for achieving Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability. Specifically, we introduce a unified adversarial discriminator that jointly performs real-distribution alignment, inter-step consistency alignment, and first-frame quality enhancement, enabling flexible sampling while preserving one-step generation quality. Furthermore, we propose a training-free adaptive long-term memory selection mechanism that selects historical states based on global semantic relevance and local value contribution, while remapping their temporal positions to preserve temporal relationships within the memory cache, thereby enabling stable one-step long-video generation. Experiments demonstrate that Any Forcing achieves high-quality and stable one-step autoregressive video generation, achieving 20.9 FPS and 24.7 FPS for short and long video generation on a single H100 GPU, respectively.

</details>

## 4. GeoFlip: Benchmarking Geometry-Sensitive Physics in Video Generation

**中文标题**: GeoFlip: Benchmarking Geometry-Sensitive Physics in Video Generation

**作者**: Yuqi Li, Junming Liu, Bowen Hu, Ruize Niu, Huiran Duan et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.42002v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42002v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42002v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry? We introduce GeoFlip, a video generation benchmark centered on physical outcome flips induced by geometry. With shared physical settings and textual prompts held fixed, GeoFlip applies controlled interventions to support extent, object placement, and mechanism configuration, constructing paired scenes with contrasting reference events, such as remaining stable versus falling or tipping. These pairs test whether models translate geometric differences in the first frame into correct physical evolution. The benchmark comprises 482 scene pairs and 964 reference videos across six physical categories, with reference outcomes established through procedural scene construction, physics simulation, and numerical validation and verification of physical events. We evaluate 16 models for image to video generation along three dimensions: event correctness, structural integrity, and similarity to reference videos. We examine evaluation consistency with independent vision language judges. On the 442 conditions shared by all models, the highest event correctness averaged across tasks is 27.98%, revealing substantial limitations in physical generation conditioned on geometry. Model strengths vary across physical mechanisms, and the three evaluation dimensions capture distinct capabilities. Typical failures include ignoring geometric changes, generating incorrect object motion, bending rigid objects, and introducing additional components. Rank correlations between judges reach 0.906 for event correctness and 0.929 for structural integrity. By combining controlled geometric interventions, verifiable physical references, and multidimensional evaluation, GeoFlip assesses progress from visual realism toward physical consistency conditioned on geometry.

</details>

## 5. TwinPast: Benchmarking Historical Evidence Use in Streaming Video Understanding with Evidence Contrastive Video Pairs

**中文标题**: TwinPast: Benchmarking Historical Evidence Use in Streaming Video Understanding with Evidence Contrastive Video Pairs

**作者**: Haotian Yang, Yong Chen, Cheng Peng, Haobo Wang, Zhongle Xie et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.41983v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41983v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41983v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

High accuracy on streaming video benchmarks does not establish whether a model uses visual history. Our review of OVO-Bench and StreamingBench finds that recent frames suffice for 32% of sampled historical questions, while models also answer many questions correctly from the question and options alone. We introduce TwinPast to test whether answers correctly follow changes in history, using 350 human-reviewed video pairs spanning five historical tasks. Each pair holds the recent video context, question, and ordered options fixed while changing critical historical evidence and the correct answer. Each video is evaluated independently, and PairAcc requires correct answers for both histories. We evaluate 34 model and input configurations. For Qwen3-VL-8B and DeepSeek-Flash, historical inputs improve performance on TwinPast, reversing the recent-frame advantage on existing benchmarks. The strongest evaluated streaming system reaches 49.14% PairAcc, compared with 66.57-91.43% for the evaluated general-purpose models. This gap persists across all five tasks. Case studies further show that models select states from the wrong time or contradict recorded event order despite retaining the relevant evidence. These findings highlight the need to connect historical memory and retrieval with reasoning that uses evidence according to the question's temporal references and event relations.

</details>

## 6. Movens-Zero: Scaling Humanoid Motion Foundation Models with Large-Scale Human Videos

**中文标题**: Movens-Zero: Scaling Humanoid Motion Foundation Models with Large-Scale Human Videos

**作者**: Wendong Bu, Tinghong Ye, Qizhou Wang, Jiacheng Li, Zhiqi Ge et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.41877v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41877v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41877v1)

**类别**: cs.RO, cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Scaling humanoid motion foundation models is constrained by the scarcity of diverse, robot-executable data and by the difficulty of jointly learning from heterogeneous multimodal inputs while supporting real-time closed-loop control. We present Movens-5M, constructed from raw Internet videos through clip generation, quality filtering, semantic annotation, motion recovery, and physical refinement with humanoid retargeting and execution verification. It contains 15 million human-motion sequences and 5 million physically executable humanoid-motion sequences spanning over 10,000 hours, aligned with text, RGB video, speech, and music. Building on this data, we introduce Movens-Zero, a scalable understanding-generation-execution architecture that supports joint learning across modalities and real-time closed-loop execution. Task-mixed flow-matching training jointly optimizes a single action expert across seven generation tasks, enabling direct motion generation from text, speech, music, and video. Multimodal Real-Time Chunking connects overlapping chunks under continuously updated instructions and robot states for continuous closed-loop execution. We further introduce Movens-Intent, a four-track benchmark with 8,192 expert-verified condition-motion pairs across text, speech, music, and video. Experiments show consistent gains across all four modalities when scaling execution-verified data from 1% to 100% and action-expert capacity from 64M to 911M parameters, while demonstrating that data quality and cross-modal supervision improve generation and execution.

</details>

## 7. SAR-TS-BERT: Physics-Guided Self-Supervised Pre-Training With Polarimetric Features for SAR Time-Series Crop Classification

**中文标题**: SAR-TS-BERT: Physics-Guided Self-Supervised Pre-Training With Polarimetric Features for SAR Time-Series Crop Classification

**作者**: Jieqiu Wang, Yuhan Zhang, Bo Zheng, Shaoming Zhang

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.41728v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41728v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41728v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Multitemporal synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imagery provides continuous, cloud-free observations of crop phenology for agricultural monitoring in cloudy and rainy regions, yet high-accuracy classification typically requires extensive labeled samples. Although self-supervised pre-training mitigates label dependency in remote sensing, existing frameworks primarily target optical imagery or generic spatial SAR scenes, underutilizing temporal backscatter dynamics and polarimetric scattering mechanisms. We propose SAR-TS-BERT, a physics-guided BERT-style self-supervised pre-training framework for SAR time-series crop classification. SAR-TS-BERT integrates backscatter intensities and polarimetric ratio features (VV, VH, and VV/VH) with dual-polarimetric Cloude-Pottier decomposition parameters ($H/α/A$) into a six-band input representation through two core designs: 1) a physics-guided span masking strategy (SpanPhysics) that adaptively selects contiguous masked spans based on temporal backscatter change rates to prioritize key phenological transitions; and 2) a dual-branch cross-modal fusion encoder that models backscatter and polarimetric decomposition features through separate Transformer branches followed by bidirectional cross-attention fusion. On independent test sets in Estonia (EuroCropsML) and the USA (California CDL), SAR-TS-BERT achieves overall accuracies (OAs) of 96.78% and 87.29%, respectively, and attains a mean monthly OA of 99.37% on Chongming Island, Shanghai. On the Estonia benchmark, pre-training improves OA and Macro F1 by 1.17 and 1.59 percentage points over training from scratch under full supervision, and by 2.58 and 5.19 percentage points using only 1% labeled data. Incorporating SAR scattering physics into temporal self-supervised pre-training effectively enhances crop classification accuracy, parcel-level mapping consistency, and few-shot transferability.

</details>

## 8. TADP: Dual-Branch Temporally Adaptive Differential Privacy for Expression-Aware Video Anonymization

**中文标题**: TADP: Dual-Branch Temporally Adaptive Differential Privacy for Expression-Aware Video Anonymization

**作者**: Feng Xu, Xun Li, Lars Petersson, Yulei Sui, David Ahmedt-Aristizabal et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.41642v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41642v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41642v1)

**类别**: cs.CV

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

We present TADP, a temporally adaptive differential privacy (DP) framework for inference-time privacy-preserving video generation under fixed-length clip-replacement adjacency. TADP uses a dual-branch design to extract identity and expression representations, clips and privatizes both branches with temporally adaptive Gaussian mechanisms whose noise scales depend only on preceding privatized releases within the same clip and are bounded below by history-uniform calibrated minima, and accounts for their joint release via adaptive Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP) composition. The clip-level RDP guarantee is converted to $(\varepsilon,δ)$-differential privacy, while soft quantization and subsequent temporal processing are applied only after privatization as DP post-processing. Experiments on facial expression datasets show reduced identity leakage under evaluated attacks, usable perceptual quality and temporal stability, and high facial expression recognition utility relative to the evaluated privacy-preserving baselines. TADP formally satisfies clip-level DP and empirically provides a favorable privacy--utility trade-off under the evaluated threat models and datasets.

</details>

## 9. CausalWAM: Learning the Causes of Actions in World Action Models

**中文标题**: CausalWAM: Learning the Causes of Actions in World Action Models

**作者**: Juntao Cheng, Jinzhou Tang, Fan Feng, Henrik I. Christensen, Yujia Zheng

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.42096v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42096v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42096v1)

**类别**: cs.AI, cs.RO, stat.ML

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

World action models (WAMs) learn latent variables from observations and use these to both predict the evolution of the scene and to generate actions. The prediction has to be of the whole scene, whereas only a portion of the scene causes actions. WAMs fitted to demonstrations do not, in general, guarantee that they will generate actions solely from the latent causes of the demonstrated actions (the action-relevant latent variables), as the model could just as well learn to imitate in this way whilst entangling the action-relevant latent variables with the nuisance variables. We therefore separate out what causes action from what prediction depends on. We prove that under a sparsity constraint the latent variables used to generate actions are an invertible function of the action-relevant latent variables alone and the true structure is identified up to a permutation of the latent variables. This requires neither sufficient temporal dependence nor any assumptions on how the unobserved regimes evolve and holds even when the action-relevant latent variables are statistically dependent on the rest. We therefore propose CWAM (Causal WAM), which incorporates the constraint into a pretrained WAM by adding a sparse routing path to its action head, with the world modeling objective remaining unchanged. Experiments on both synthetic data and real-world robots validate the theory as well as showcase the improved efficiency in closed-loop success.

</details>

## 10. Chronos-2: A foundation model for multivariate time series forecasting

**中文标题**: Chronos-2: A foundation model for multivariate time series forecasting

**作者**: Abdul Fatir Ansari, Oleksandr Shchur, Jaris Küken, Andreas Auer, Boran Han et al.

**发布日期**: 2026-10-30

**arXiv ID**: [2610.41956v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41956v1)

**PDF**: [下载链接](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41956v1)

**类别**: cs.LG, cs.AI, stat.ML

<details>
<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Accurate forecasting with uncertainty quantification is critical in a variety of scientific applications ranging from epidemiology and economics to energy systems and earth sciences. Yet most forecasting models still need to be developed and trained separately for each domain-specific dataset. Time series foundation models (TSFMs) aim to remove this limitation and enable the application of the same model across domains through large-scale pretraining. However, most existing TSFMs treat each time series independently and therefore cannot model interactions among time series and external drivers. Here, we introduce Chronos-2, a foundation model for zero-shot forecasting that models the interactions between multivariate time series and heterogeneous covariates, which commonly arise in scientific applications. The model infers relationships among time series directly from historical context and generates probabilistic forecasts without task-specific treatment. Across various benchmarks, Chronos-2 achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on average by improving skill scores by 5.2 percentage points relative to the strongest baselines and increasing win rates by 10 percentage points. Chronos-2 is also computationally efficient, generating forecasts for 100 time series in under one second on a single mid-range GPU, 50x and 1000x faster end-to-end than the best task-specific point and probabilistic forecasters, respectively. In addition, we demonstrate its effectiveness in diverse scientific settings -- including infectious-disease forecasting, macroeconomic modeling, carbon-flux prediction, and runoff simulation -- where the model matches or surpasses specialized methods developed for each domain. These results show that a single pretrained model can capture structure shared across temporal systems.

</details>

<summary><b>摘要 (Abstract)</b></summary>

Data Assimilation (DA) reconstructs the state of a spatio-temporal system by combining sensor observations with the output of a numerical model. Since model states are high-dimensional while observations cover only a small fraction of the domain, DA is a challenging large-scale estimation problem, central to weather forecasting and oceanography. Classical variational methods such as 3D-Var rely on a hand-crafted background error covariance that assumes unbiased errors, and therefore cannot capture state-dependent correlations or systematic model biases. The learned covariance can also replace the hand-crafted one in 3D-Var, improving its accuracy without changing the variational formulation. Experiments on sea surface temperature and salinity, with both synthetic and real, extremely sparse in-situ observations, show that the framework outperforms 3D-Var, a classical GP and a competing DL model, while reducing inference time by more than 98% with respect to 3D-Var.
